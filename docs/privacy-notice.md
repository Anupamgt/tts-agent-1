# Privacy notice (draft for the clinic and its lawyer)

Status: draft, 29 Oct 2026 (decision log D-06 to D-09). Not legal advice. The clinic and its lawyer review this before the first real patient call. Text in [brackets] is filled in per clinic. The buildathon demo uses a fictional clinic and needs no notice.

Basis: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and Rules 2025 (notice, consent, security and breach duties from 13 May 2027), and until then the IT Act's SPDI Rules 2011, which treat health information as sensitive personal data.

## 1. Spoken opening (first sentence of every call, cannot be skipped)

- **English:** "Hello, you have reached [Clinic name]. I am the clinic's automated assistant. Calls are recorded for quality and safety. You can ask for the desk at any time."
- **Hindi (native speaker to review):** "नमस्ते, आपने [क्लिनिक का नाम] को कॉल किया है। मैं क्लिनिक का स्वचालित सहायक हूँ। कॉल गुणवत्ता और सुरक्षा के लिए रिकॉर्ड की जाती हैं। आप कभी भी डेस्क से बात करने के लिए कह सकते हैं।"
- **Hinglish:** the agent uses the English or Hindi line, whichever the caller starts in.

If the caller objects to the recording, the agent says it cannot turn recording off on this line and offers a callback from the desk (P3, or P2 if the request is for today).

## 3. Full notice (web page at the link)

**Who is responsible.** [Clinic name, address] decides how your details are used (the "data fiduciary"). Bolnai runs the phone assistant and console for the clinic and uses your details only on the clinic's instructions, under a written contract (a "data processor"). Bolnai does not use your calls for any purpose of its own, including training AI models.

**What we collect.**
- Your phone number, name, age or date of birth, and patient ID.
- Your bookings, payments and refunds with the clinic.
- What you say on calls to the assistant: a recording and a written transcript.
- Messages you leave for a doctor, and medicine refill requests (the medicine name you read out, the doctor, days left).
- For a child: the same details, given by a parent or guardian.

We do not ask for Aadhaar, ABHA, your address or detailed medical information on a call.

**Why.**
- To book, change or cancel visits, and tell you about delays.
- To answer questions about the clinic.
- To pass your messages and refill requests to your own doctor. The assistant never gives medical advice and never decides on medicines.
- To send confirmations and, if you agree, reminders by SMS and payment links by WhatsApp.
- To keep the service safe: checking who is calling, stopping misuse, fixing problems.
- In an emergency, to alert clinic staff. The assistant tells you to call 112 or 108.

**Who else handles your details.**
- Bolna, the voice platform, and its speech and language-model providers: [list each provider and the country where it processes].
- The SMS provider [name], and WhatsApp (Meta) only if you agreed to WhatsApp messages.
- The clinic's payment gateway [name], only for payments and refunds.
- The clinic's booking calendar holds only your first name, last initial and arrival window.

Your details are stored in a database in India. Some providers above may process call audio outside India, as listed.

**How long we keep it.**

| What | Kept for |
|---|---|
| Call recordings | 30 days |
| Call transcripts | 180 days |
| Records of who accessed your details | 1 year |
| Refill requests and messages to doctors, with the doctor's reply | 3 years |
| Bookings and payments | As long as the clinic's accounts require |

**Your choices and rights.**
- Stop reminders or WhatsApp messages at any time: reply STOP, or tell the desk.
- Ask what we hold about you, ask for a correction, or ask us to delete it where we do not need to keep it.
- Name someone to act for you if you cannot.
- Complain to the clinic's contact below. If you are not satisfied, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

**If something goes wrong.** If your details are exposed, the clinic will tell you what happened, what it means for you and what you can do, without delay.
