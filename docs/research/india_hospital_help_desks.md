## 1. What do NABH standards say about patient information, help desks, patient rights, grievance redressal and patient feedback? Which chapters/standards apply?

### Takeaway
The NABH Hospital Standards, 6th Edition (effective 1 Jan 2025), never use the term "help desk". The help desk's functions are instead spread across two chapters:
- **AAC (Access, Assessment and Continuity of Care):** display of services, registration and unique ID.
- **PRE (Patient Rights and Education):**
  - PRE.1: rights displayed
  - PRE.6: tariff and expected-cost information
  - PRE.7: feedback and complaint redressal
  - PRE.8: communication

The 6th edition adds an emphasis on Patient-Reported Experience Measures (PREMs).

### Cited Findings
**Edition and AAC (primary source: the NABH standards PDF)**
- The current book is the NABH Accreditation Standards for Hospitals, 6th Edition, effective 1 January 2025 — [NABH 6th Ed. PDF](https://portal.nabh.co/images/Standards/NABH%20Hospital%20Accreditation%20Standard%206th%20Edition%20January%202025.pdf)
- AAC.1: "The organisation defines and displays the healthcare services that it provides." Objective element AAC.1.d: "Defined clinical services are prominently displayed" (Commitment level) — [NABH 6th Ed. PDF](https://portal.nabh.co/images/Standards/NABH%20Hospital%20Accreditation%20Standard%206th%20Edition%20January%202025.pdf)
- AAC.2: "The organisation has a well-defined registration and admission process."
  - (a) Written guidance for registering and admitting patients.
  - (b) A unique identification number is generated at the end of registration (CORE).
  - (c) Patients are accepted only if the service can be provided.
  - (d) Guidance covers managing patients when no bed is available.
  - (e) Access is prioritised by clinical need.
  - The 6th-edition change log adds "general consent" to the AAC.2.a interpretation.
  - Source: [NABH 6th Ed. PDF](https://portal.nabh.co/images/Standards/NABH%20Hospital%20Accreditation%20Standard%206th%20Edition%20January%202025.pdf)
- The only signage requirement found in the readable portion is AAC.9.k: "Imaging signage is prominently displayed in all appropriate locations" — [NABH 6th Ed. PDF](https://portal.nabh.co/images/Standards/NABH%20Hospital%20Accreditation%20Standard%206th%20Edition%20January%202025.pdf)

**PRE changes from the 5th to the 6th edition (primary source: change log)**
- The PRE intent now emphasises PREMs and patient engagement.
- PRE.3 elements a, b and c were merged.
- The PRE.4.a interpretation adds the HIV and AIDS Act 2017.
- PRE.5.j is a new element on patient engagement to improve clinical outcomes.
- The PRE.7.b interpretation explains PREMs.
- The PRE.8.c interpretation expands SPIKES (the breaking-bad-news protocol).
- Source: [NABH 6th Ed. PDF](https://portal.nabh.co/images/Standards/NABH%20Hospital%20Accreditation%20Standard%206th%20Edition%20January%202025.pdf)

**PRE headings, 6th edition (secondary source: a slide deck by Dr J. L. Meena that reproduces the standards)**

Source for all rows: [SlideShare, PRE NABH 6th Std Jan 2025](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/patient-rights-and-education-pre-nabh-6-std-jan-2025-pdf/277663013)

**PRE objective elements (same secondary source: [SlideShare, PRE NABH 6th Std](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/patient-rights-and-education-pre-nabh-6-std-jan-2025-pdf/277663013))**
- PRE.1.a: rights and responsibilities are "documented, displayed and they are made aware of the same."
- PRE.1.b: rights and responsibilities are "actively promoted."
- PRE.2.h: rights include "a right to complain and information on how to voice a complaint."
- PRE.6, which is help-desk and billing-counter territory:
  - (a) Patients are made aware of the pricing policy in different settings.
  - (b) "The relevant tariff list is available to patients."
  - (c) Expected costs are explained.
  - (d) Financial implications are explained when the care plan changes.
- PRE.7:
  - (a) A mechanism to capture feedback, including patient satisfaction.
  - (b) A mechanism to capture patient experience.
  - (c) Complaints are redressed as per the defined mechanism.
  - (d) Patients are told how to give feedback or lodge complaints.
  - (e) Feedback and complaints are reviewed and analysed "within a defined time frame". NABH sets no number; each hospital defines its own.
  - (f) Corrective and preventive action follows.
- PRE.5.a: education is given "in a language and format that they can understand."

**How hospitals apply PRE in practice**
- Malabar Medical College Hospital's PRE policy (rev. May 2022, referencing the 2014 NABH Pre-Accreditation Entry Level standards) [OLDER]:
  - Rights and responsibilities are displayed "at convenient places in the hospital".
  - They are communicated "in a format and language that they understand".
  - Listed rights include information on expected cost and "how to voice a complaint".
  - Source: [MMC Chapter 4 PRE](https://connect.mmchospital.in/assets/scope_of_service/4._MMC-Chapter-4_PRE.pdf)
- Sri Kiran hospital's rights display "as per NABH norms" includes "the right to have information on expected cost of the treatment" and the right to make complaints or suggestions — [Sri Kiran](https://www.srikiran.org/?p=2036)

**NABH's own complaints process (against accredited hospitals)**
- Policy & Procedure for Handling Complaints, Issue 7 (08/2025):
  - Complaints are accepted only through the QCI "Quality Setu" portal.
  - Anonymous complaints are not acted on.
  - Complaints are disposed of within 90 days.
  - Billing/financial complaints, clinical negligence complaints and complaints against individual health workers are not accepted.
  - Source: [NABH Complaints Policy Issue 7](https://testing.nabh.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NABH-Policy-Procedure-for-Handling-Complaints-Issue-7_Protected.pdf)

**Other NABH-related items**
- A vendor blog claims "NABH guidelines require acknowledging complaints within 15 days and resolving them within 30 days" — [Zonka Feedback blog, Dec 2024, updated Feb 2026](https://www.zonkafeedback.com/blog/streamline-complaint-management-in-nabh-certified-hospitals). This is unverified. The standards text reviewed says only "defined time frame" ([SlideShare PRE](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/patient-rights-and-education-pre-nabh-6-std-jan-2025-pdf/277663013)).
- NABH's 6th-edition PRE masterclass (01.12.2026, INR 1,180) covers displaying and protecting rights, informed consent, patient education, complaint and grievance mechanisms with timely resolution, and privacy — [NABH Masterclass](https://nabh.co/training/hco-full-accreditation-masterclass-6th-edition-chapter-4-patient-rights-and-education-pre/)
- PM-JAY pays empanelled hospitals a 10% incentive for entry-level NABH and 15% for full NABH accreditation — [Lok Sabha AU744, 29 Nov 2024](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2983521/1/AU744_tZHjHu.pdf)

### Inferences
- NABH-accredited hospitals typically put several things at the front desk or help desk:
  - the rights and responsibilities display (PRE.1)
  - the tariff list and cost estimates (PRE.6)
  - feedback forms and complaint intake (PRE.7)
  - registration and unique ID generation (AAC.2)
  - the list of services (AAC.1)
- NABH leaves complaint timelines to each hospital. Internal SLAs (e.g., 24h/7d) are hospital policy, not NABH requirements.
- The new PREM emphasis is likely to push help desks and patient relations teams toward structured experience surveys rather than simple satisfaction forms.

### Gaps
- I could not get the official PRE chapter text from NABH's own PDF; the fetch was truncated to the AAC chapter. The PRE wording above comes from a secondary slide deck and should be checked against the official book.
- No NABH standard explicitly requires a "help desk" or "information desk". Whether NABH assessor guidance (the interpretation text) mentions one could not be confirmed.

## 2. What are Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY help desks, and what does the Pradhan Mantri Arogya Mitra (PMAM) do at empanelled hospitals?

### Takeaway
Every PM-JAY empanelled hospital must provide a separate PM-JAY kiosk or help desk at reception. It is staffed by a PMAM (also called the "non-medical coordinator"), working with a doctor designated as the medical coordinator (MEDCO).

The PMAM is the beneficiary's single point of contact across the whole episode:
- eligibility check and identity verification (BIS)
- Ayushman card creation
- TMS registration and pre-authorisation uploads
- photos at admission, during treatment and at discharge
- discharge documents and claim documents
- post-discharge entitlement information and feedback
- escalation of grievances

Grievances follow a separate three-tier system: DGNO, then SGNO, then the CGRMS portal and the 14555 helpline. Its timelines are often missed.

### Cited Findings
**Mandate and set-up (NHA empanelment guidelines, Dec 2021)**
- Hospitals must provide a separate kiosk for beneficiary management at the reception area — [NHA Empanelment Guidelines Dec 2021](https://nha.gov.in/img/resources/Hospital-Empanelment-Guidelines-21-12-22.pdf)
- A "non-medical coordinator" acts as concierge and help desk for visiting patients. The role covers pre-authorisation, claim settlement and follow-up, and managing the kiosk.
- A designated medical coordinator submits pre-authorisations and claims and follows up on queries.
- Required IT kit: desktop/laptop with internet, printer, webcam, scanner/fax and a biometric device.
- Hospitals must promote PM-JAY on their premises with banners and brochures.
- In Aspirational Districts, a desk is required at the main entrance.
- The guidelines set no staff ratio and no 24x7 rule for the kiosk.
- Source for all of the above: [NHA Empanelment Guidelines Dec 2021](https://nha.gov.in/img/resources/Hospital-Empanelment-Guidelines-21-12-22.pdf)
- The NHA Ready Reckoner restates the reception kiosk staffed by an "AB-PMJAY non-medical coordinator" plus a medical coordinator who keeps follow-up records — [NHA Ready Reckoner](https://nha.gov.in/img/resources/Ready-Reckoner.pdf)

**Job role and qualification**
- The PMAM is an NSQF Level 3 qualification (NQR 2021/HLT/HSSC/04383, Healthcare Sector Skill Council), designed for NHA — [NQR PMAM](https://www.nqr.gov.in/qualifications/1753)
  - Described as the "primary contact for the beneficiaries at every empanelled hospital care provider".
  - Identifies and verifies beneficiaries using the Beneficiary Identification System (BIS).
  - Runs Transaction Management System (TMS) processes: pre-authorisation and claims.
  - Mandatory occupational standards include running help desk operations, giving scheme information and checking eligibility.
  - Entry: 12th pass. Duration: 45 hours.
  - Listed "Valid Till 31/12/2024", so current status is unclear.
  - Progression: Hospital Front Desk Coordinator (Level 4), then Patient Relations Associate (Level 5).
- An MSDE document is titled "Skill India supports Ayushman Bharat – PMJAY; To train One Lakh Arogya Mitras" (2019) [OLDER] — [MSDE](https://skilldevelopment.gov.in/sites/default/files/2019-09/Skill%20India%20supports%20Ayushman%20Bharat%20-%20Pradhan%20Mantri%20Jan%20Arogya%20Yojana%3B%20To%20train%20One%20Lakh%20Arogya%20Mitras.pdf). Only the title was seen; the content was not reviewed.

**Original Ayushman Mitra guidelines, 2018 [OLDER] (secondary summary: [TaxHeal, Sept 2018](https://taxheal.com/ayushman-mitra.html))**
- Staffing by average daily cases: 0–10 cases, 1 Mitra; 10–20, 2; 20–30, 3; 30–40, 4. States may revise these numbers but must ensure 24x7 availability.
- Desk near reception. Equipment: PC, printer, scanner, camera, webcam, QR reader. A dedicated broadband line of 2 Mbps or more. A standard uniform.
- In public hospitals, the State Health Agency recruits directly or through an agency, and may pay from Rogi Kalyan Samiti funds. In private hospitals, the hospital recruits and pays.
- Eligibility: 10+2 plus certification. Preference for women and ASHAs. One-year renewable term.
- Tasks:
  - receive the patient at the kiosk and collect Aadhaar or other ID plus the family card
  - search the database, take a photo and validate the mobile number
  - print the e-card after approval (about 30 minutes)
  - coordinate admission and bed availability
  - explain that OPD care is not covered
  - collect claim documents and verify the discharge summary
  - escalate grievances to the Grievance Cell or District Coordinator
  - track money collected in violation of the rules (for refunds)
  - help holders of lost cards

**Step-by-step process flow (Uttarakhand SHA, dated 6-5-2019) [OLDER] — [PM-JAY Process Flow](https://sha.uk.gov.in/ScannedDocs/WebSite/Uploads/CLAIM_ADJ/pm-jay-process-flow-at-empanelled-hospitals-6-5-19.pdf)**
1. The registration desk runs the "Am I Eligible" check, or the patient uses the kiosk or calls 14555. Eligible patients go to the PM-JAY kiosk.
2. The PMAM verifies identity through BIS and creates the e-card ("golden record") if the patient has none.
3. The PMAM explains that care is free only if hospitalised, and gives cost estimates for non-admitted diagnostics.
4. The PMAM selects the package recommended by the specialist or MEDCO and uploads documents to TMS. Where insurer/trust approval is needed, it comes within 6 hours at most.
5. The PMAM photographs the beneficiary on the bed at admission, again during or after treatment, and at discharge.
6. At discharge, the PMAM collects the discharge summary, clinical notes and scans.
7. The PMAM tells the patient about free post-discharge medicines and diagnostics for up to 15 days.
8. The PMAM collects feedback on a standard form. The call centre separately surveys a sample of discharged patients.

**Operations Manual for hospitals (April 2022) [OLDER] — [NHA Operations Manual](https://nha.gov.in/img/resources/Operation%20Manual%20for%20AB%20PM-JAY.pdf)**
- PMAMs or Common Service Centres act as "makers" in BIS. Search fields include name, ration card, mobile, RSBY URN and state scheme ID.
- The PMAM or MEDCO registers the patient in TMS and raises the pre-authorisation. The Pre-auth Panel Doctor approves, rejects or queries the PMAM.
- After treatment, the PMAM updates TMS and raises the claim, which the Claim Panel Doctor reviews.
- Claim documents should be uploaded within 24 hours of discharge.

**Grievance system (NHA Grievance Redressal Guidelines, Dec 2021) [OLDER] — [NHA Grievance Guidelines Dec 2021](https://nha.gov.in/img/resources/OM-Grievance-Redressal-Guideline-Dec-2021.pdf)**
- Officers and committees:
  - District and State Grievance Nodal Officers (DGNO and SGNO).
  - District Grievance Redressal Committee, chaired by the District Magistrate.
  - State Grievance Redressal Committee, chaired by the SHA CEO.
  - State Appellate Authority: the Principal Secretary (Health).
- Channels: the CGRMS portal and the 14555 helpline. Each complaint gets a unique grievance number.
- Timelines:
  - SOS (during hospitalisation): resolved within 6 working hours.
  - Non-SOS: 15 days.
  - Committee decisions: 30 days.
  - Target: at least 98% resolved on time.
- Listed SOS grievances against hospitals include:
  - denial of treatment
  - demanding money for free services
  - not returning the card at discharge
  - outside prescriptions for free items
  - charging for printing the Ayushman card
  - "Arogya Mitra" non-availability, non-cooperation or misconduct
- Common Service Centres may charge Rs 30 for a card.

**Scale and performance**
- As of 25 Nov 2024, 18,184 grievances were registered on CGRMS. Categories included denial of treatment, charges for admission, discharge or medicines, and "unavailability of PMAM" — [Lok Sabha AU1885, 6 Dec 2024](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2999990/1/AU1885_LctFE3.pdf)
- CAG's PM-JAY performance audit (2023) found:
  - Of 37,903 grievances, only 3,718 (9.80%) were redressed within the turnaround time.
  - SHA Chhattisgarh had redressed none of its 40.
  - PMAMs were "not rotated frequently" in Himachal Pradesh and Tamil Nadu.
  - Source: [CAG PM-JAY Audit 2023](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2023/04_Executive-Summary-064d22bab3b3f45.25634714.pdf)
- Government description (Nov 2024): PMAMs are deployed at empanelled hospitals to guide beneficiaries and coordinate with doctors. "Uniform kiosks" are the first point of contact for awareness, card creation and access to treatment. About 14 lakh Ayushman Vay Vandana (70+) cards had been created as of 25 Nov 2024. Helplines: 14555 and 1800-110-770 — [Lok Sabha AU744](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2983521/1/AU744_tZHjHu.pdf)
- 30,957 hospitals were empanelled as of 1 Mar 2025 (17,091 public, 13,866 private) — [Lok Sabha AS404, 28 Mar 2025](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/3009739/1/AS404_w9lAPq.pdf)

**Examples in practice**
- AIIMS New Delhi:
  - The "Ayushman Bharat Kendra" is near Gate No. 1, opposite the RAK OPD, and runs 24x7 with PMAMs and PCMs round the clock.
  - PMAMs handle registration, eligibility and document checks, demographic authentication, and "query and grievance handling", supervised by PCMs and Medical Social Service Officers (MSSOs).
  - Only admitted and day-care patients are covered; OPD is not.
  - The golden card is free.
  - Source: [AIIMS AB-PMJAY booklet](https://www.aiims.edu/images/pdf/notice/Ayushman%20Bharat_27-10-21.pdf)
- Odisha (13 Aug 2026):
  - All AB-PMJAY/GJAY empanelled hospitals must run a 24x7 AB-PMJAY help desk at a prominent spot near the main entrance, staffed by enough PMAMs to cover round the clock.
  - Hospitals must also deliver PM-RAHAT cashless emergency care for road-accident victims (up to Rs 1.5 lakh for 7 days, if they arrive within 24 hours of the accident).
  - Hospitals must integrate their hospital software and ABHA records with ABDM by 31 Aug 2026, or lose incentives and risk de-empanelment.
  - Sample help desk designs were shown at the review meeting.
  - Sources: [OdishaBytes](https://odishabytes.com/odisha-asks-empaneled-hospitals-to-set-up-24x7-help-desks-provide-cashless-pm-rahat-treatment/amp/); [UNI](https://test.uniindia.com/news/east/health-odisha-ayushman-help-desks/3942304.html)
- Odisha state scheme (BSKY, Nov 2022) [OLDER]: each government medical college hospital nominates a Senior Nursing Officer as "Swasthya Sanjoak" to update families on in-patients, including ICU and HDU patients. "Swasthya Sahayaks" already work in 402 private empanelled hospitals — [Zee/ANI](https://zee5.com/articles/odisha-to-strengthen-help-desks-in-govt-medical-colleges-hospitals)
- Integrity risk: in March 2025 a data operator at the HIMCARE (Himachal state scheme) counter in PGIMER's Nehru Hospital was arrested in an Ayushman Bharat cashless-medicines fraud worth about Rs 60,000 at the AMRIT pharmacy — [Medical Dialogues, 18 Mar 2025](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/health/hospital-diagnostics/pgi-chandigarh-staffer-held-in-ayushman-fraud-case-145073)
- Antecedent: West Bengal's 2014 order made Rogi Sahayata Kendras the RSBY help desk in public hospitals (admission, hospital transaction system, discharge). Staff were Rogi Kalyan Samiti hires paid Rs 6,500 a month; caps were 5 per district or sub-district hospital and 8 per medical college hospital [OLDER] — [WB Order 26 Feb 2014](https://wbxpress.com/management-rsby-help-desk-govt-hospitals/)

### Inferences
- The PMAM desk is effectively a scheme-specific front office, often separate from the hospital's own enquiry or registration counter. In public hospitals it sits under the Medical Social Service or administration structure (AIIMS model).
- PMAM performance shows up directly in grievance categories ("unavailability of PMAM", "money demanded"). That makes the desk both the patient's advocate and a compliance point for the scheme.
- States (e.g., Odisha 2026) are tightening rules toward 24x7 desks at the main entrance and folding in other entitlements (PM-RAHAT) and ABDM tasks. The PMAM's remit is widening beyond PM-JAY claims.

### Gaps
- The current official NHA PMAM guideline (post-2018) setting staffing norms was not found. The 2018 ratios come from a secondary summary.
- The NHA IEC Guidelines v2.0 (2021-22) have a "Hospital Branding Guidelines" section (kiosk design, signage), but that section could not be read: [NHA IEC Guidelines](https://nha.gov.in/img/resources/1623-NHA-IEC-Guidelines-3-01-21.pdf).
- No national count of deployed PMAMs was found.
- An empirical study ([BMC Health Services Research, s12913-021-07448-3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-021-07448-3)) could not be fetched.

## 3. What do government hospital help desks, "May I Help You" desks and enquiry counters do? What do Kayakalp, LaQshya and NQAS require?

### Takeaway
NQAS is the standard that explicitly requires an enquiry or help desk in public facilities. Under Area of Concern B (Patient Rights), it requires:
- an "Enquiry Desk with dedicated staff" fluent in the local language (ME B1.7)
- uniform signage in the local language
- a citizen charter that includes the help desk number and the grievance process
- a complaint box with a defined redressal process
- monthly patient satisfaction surveys (Area G)

Kayakalp covers only signage. LaQshya treats a grievance help desk as a feedback channel alongside Mera Aspataal.

In practice, government help desks combine several jobs:
- enquiry and wayfinding
- medical social work: free medicines, Rashtriya Arogya Nidhi, railway concessions, PM-JAY
- control-room grievance handling
- scheme kiosks

CAG audits show frequent shortfalls.

### Cited Findings
**NQAS (NHSRC)**
- Eight Areas of Concern: Service Provision, Patient Rights, Inputs, Support Services, Clinical Care, Infection Control, Quality Management, Outcome. The standards are ISQua-accredited and cover District Hospitals, CHCs, PHCs and urban PHCs — [NHSRC NQAS page](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/national-quality-assurance-standards)
- NQAS checklist measurable elements (MusQan/CHC assessment tool, version not stated) — [NQAS MusQan tool](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/MusQan%20National%20Quality%20Assurance%20Standards%20And%20Assessment%20Tools%20For%20DH%20And%20CHC.pdf):
  - **ME B1.1:** directional signage.
  - **ME B1.2:** services, entitlements and grievance redressal displayed.
  - **ME B1.3:** "Display of citizen charter", which should include a "Help desk number" and the "Complaints and Grievances Mechanism".
  - **ME B1.6:** signage and information in the local language.
  - **ME B1.7:** "Availability of Enquiry Desk with dedicated staff", with staff "fluent in local language".
  - **ME B4.5:** "Availability of complaint box and display of process for grievance redressal". The assessor checks the process "from complaint registration till its resolution".
  - **ME G2.1:** "Client satisfaction survey is done on monthly basis".
  - **ME G2.2:** feedback analysis and root cause analysis.
  - **ME G2.3:** action plans.
- An NHSRC slide deck on Area of Concern B lists implementation points:
  - B1: branding, citizen charter, signage (facility name and timings "visible in night", "Uniform Color, User friendly and Local Language"), IEC, and information on entitlements.
  - B3: display of patient rights and responsibilities; a grievance and complaint handling mechanism.
  - B5: free services.
  - Source: [NHSRC AoC B deck](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2025-07/Area%20of%20Concern%20B.pdf)

**Kayakalp (2024 revised tool)**
- Only signage is relevant: A4.4, "All signages (directional & departmental) are in local language and follow uniform colour scheme." The tool has no checkpoints on help desks, citizen charters, grievances or patient feedback — [Kayakalp Revised Tool 2024](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2024-08/Kayakalp_Revised_Tool-2024.pdf)

**LaQshya (labour rooms; guideline marked 2017) [OLDER]**
- Intervention 10 lists a "Grievance Redressal Help Desk" alongside "MeraAspataal or manual recording" as channels for beneficiary feedback.
- Checkpoints B1.1 (uniform, user-friendly signage) and B1.2 (display of services and entitlements).
- Target: 80% of beneficiaries satisfied or highly satisfied.
- Source: [LaQshya Guidelines (NHM HP copy)](https://nhm.hp.gov.in/storage/app/media/uploaded-files/LaQshya%20Guidelines%20.pdf)

**Mera Aspataal (MoHFW patient feedback system)**
- Launched 29 Aug 2016. Channels: SMS, outbound dialling, app and web — [NHSRC Mera Aspataal](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-04/Mera_Asptaal.pdf)
- Feedback in 7 languages for hospitals visited in the last 7 days. Results go to facility, district, state and national dashboards — [Mera Aspataal About](https://meraaspataal.mohfw.gov.in/about_us)
- A DO letter from the AS&MD (NHM), dated 7 Nov 2020, asked states to:
  - nominate a nodal officer
  - integrate all public facilities
  - act on the reports
  - The letter calls "Patient Satisfaction Score" a key criterion for NQAS certification. As of 29.10.2020, 6,074 facilities were integrated, including 710 private ones [OLDER].
  - Source: [Mera Aspataal DO letter](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-01/Mera%20Aspataal%20DO%20letter%2007092020.pdf)

**AIIMS New Delhi**
- Facilities listed in its general information page — [AIIMS General Info](https://aiims.edu/aiims/hosp-serv/citizen-charter/general-inf.htm):
  - Central Enquiry & Registration office inside the main entrance.
  - "OPD enquiry counters and help desks" at OPD locations.
  - Medical Social Services in Room 9 (MSSOs and Social Guides).
  - A 24-hour Control Room, where duty officers handle administrative problems and "public grievances" about patient care.
  - LED scroll displays and guide maps.
- At the R.P. Centre (ophthalmology), the "May I Help You Counter" is staffed by part-time social guides. The Medical Social Service unit provides — [AIIMS RPC MSS page](https://www.aiims.edu/index.php/en/notices/256-rpc_25_8):
  - free medicines and spectacles for poor patients
  - help with accommodation
  - financial aid through Rashtriya Arogya Nidhi and the PM's Relief Fund
  - PM-JAY coordination
  - railway concession and disability certificates
  - levy exemption for BPL patients
  - (page updated Dec 2025)

## 5. Are there MoHFW or NHM guidelines on patient help desks or grievance redressal, and what is the Charter of Patients' Rights (NHRC)?

### Takeaway
The NHRC drafted a 17-right Charter of Patients' Rights (2018). MoHFW sent a subset (rights i–xiii) to States/UTs on 2 June 2019. The National Council for Clinical Establishments later approved further rights (xiv–xx) for inclusion in the Minimum Standards; the charter was updated 23 Aug 2021.

The charter requires hospitals to:
- display rates in English and the local language
- name a grievance redressal officer and display their contact details
- keep grievance records

The draft also set 24-hour acknowledgement and 15-day written-outcome timelines. MoHFW/NHM operational norms come from:
- NQAS
- the NHM Assessor's Guidebook (2013)
- IPHS 2022
- Mera Aspataal

### Cited Findings
**NHRC draft charter (via MoHFW, undated)**
- The 17 rights are — [MoHFW Patient Charter draft](https://main.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/PatientCharterforcomments.pdf):
  1. information
  2. records and reports
  3. emergency care
  4. informed consent
  5. confidentiality, dignity and privacy
  6. second opinion
  7. transparency in rates
  8. non-discrimination
  9. safety and quality
  10. choosing alternative treatment
  11. choosing where to get medicines and tests
  12. proper referral
  13. protection in clinical trials
  14. protection in research
  15. discharge, or receiving a body, without delay
  16. patient education
  17. "Right to be heard and seek redressal"
- Grievance provisions in the draft — [MoHFW Patient Charter draft](https://main.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/PatientCharterforcomments.pdf):
  - An "Internal Grievance Redressal Officer" must acknowledge complaints "within 24 hours by assigning a registration number".
  - Patients have the right to receive "in writing the outcome of the complaint within 15 days".
  - Next, the district registering authority mediates within 30 days.
  - Then appeal to the State Council for Clinical Establishments, which decides within 30 days.
  - The charter is to be displayed "prominently", and rates displayed "at a conspicuous place in local as well as English language".

**Adoption history and the current version**
- Scroll (2 Jan 2019) [OLDER] — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/pulse/907502/scroll_in):
  - The NHRC adopted the charter in August (2018) and MoHFW put it out for comments.
  - It recommends adoption by states that have the Clinical Establishments Act.
  - FICCI and the IMA objected, especially to the right to choose where to buy medicines and tests, and to discharge without delay.
- National Council for Clinical Establishments, "Charter of Patients' Rights and Responsibilities" (updated 23 Aug 2021) — [NCCE Charter](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-06/3181.pdf):
  - Rights (i)–(xiii) were sent to States/UTs by MoHFW letter dated 2 June 2019.
  - Rights (xiv)–(xx) were approved at the Council's 12th meeting for inclusion in the Minimum Standards.
  - Hospitals must display rates for each service in the local language and English, and display ambulance charges.
  - Hospitals must identify a grievance redressal officer and display their name and contact details in the local language and English, keep records of grievances and remedial action, and display the district registering authority's contact details.
  - States must disseminate the charter widely.
  - Patient responsibilities include paying agreed fees on time and never resorting to violence.
- Civil society view (mid-2019) [OLDER]: Jan Swasthya Abhiyan said the 2 June 2019 letter kept only about half the NHRC rights. It said the letter dropped emergency care and "the right to be heard and seek redressal", "apparently due to resistance from the private medical lobby". It also said the 17-right charter had been on the ministry website since Sept 2018 — [Counterview](https://counterview.in/2019/07/private-pressure-health-ministry-says.html?m=0)
- Possible conflict: Counterview says redressal was dropped in 2019, while the 2021 NCCE version includes grievance-officer requirements. These are consistent if redressal was restored among rights xiv–xx in 2021, but I could not confirm that from the documents.

**NHM and MoHFW operational norms**
- The NHM Assessor's Guidebook (2013) requires — [CAG UP 2019](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2019/Chapter_2_Out_patient_Services_of_Report_No_2_of_2019_Hospital_Management_in_Uttar_Pradesh_Government_of_Uttar_Pradesh.pdf):
  - the Citizen's Charter displayed at a suitable place
  - a mechanism to receive, register and dispose of complaints first-come-first-served, with an action-taken register and follow-up
  - monthly OPD patient satisfaction surveys
- The same guidebook sets a 3–5 minute registration norm — [CAG Maharashtra 2024](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2024/Chapter-3-Healthcare-Services-0676d3322b33346.02416031.pdf)
- IPHS 2022 requires at least two registration counters at rural and district hospitals, one for women, the elderly and the specially-abled (as cited by CAG) — [CAG Maharashtra 2024](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2024/Chapter-3-Healthcare-Services-0676d3322b33346.02416031.pdf)
- Mera Aspataal DO letter (7 Nov 2020): integrate all public facilities, and use the patient satisfaction score for NQAS — [DO letter](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-01/Mera%20Aspataal%20DO%20letter%2007092020.pdf)
- The Online Registration System (ORS) was inaugurated by the PM on 1 July 2015 for online OPD appointments and is ABHA-integrated. e-Hospital is a cloud HMIS for government hospitals — [MeitY/PIB doc, 15 Jan 2024](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2024/jan/doc2024115298601.pdf)

### Inferences
- No single central MoHFW "help desk guideline" applies to all hospitals. The obligations come from:
  - the Clinical Establishments Act minimum standards and the Patients' Rights Charter (grievance officer, displays)
  - NQAS (enquiry desk, citizen charter, complaint box) for public facilities
  - the Mera Aspataal and NHM guidebook norms
- In practice the help desk is where these display and complaint obligations are met.
- The charter applies through state adoption: health is a state subject, and the Clinical Establishments Act applies only in states that adopted it. Enforcement therefore varies by state.

### Gaps
- No standalone MoHFW or NHM guideline titled "patient help desk" was found.
- The full text of the 2 June 2019 MoHFW letter was not reviewed.
- Which states have formally notified the charter is not documented here.

## 6. What changed in 2023–2026 (ABHA registration, Scan and Share, AI chatbots, kiosks), and how does it affect the help desk's role?

### Inferences
- Digital registration (Scan and Share, ORS, WhatsApp bots) is taking over the help desk's oldest job, issuing OPD slips. The desk's remaining value lies in:
  - inclusion: ABHA creation for the elderly, people without smartphones and people with low literacy
  - handling exceptions: missed tokens, ID mismatches, scheme eligibility
  - escalating grievances
- ABHA-mandatory registration (e.g., G.B. Pant 2026) makes assisted ABHA creation desks essential to equitable access.
- The SARATHI rule that volunteers take no clinical or administrative roles points to a layered model: volunteers for navigation, paid staff or PMAMs for transactions, and digital tools for routine registration and feedback.

### Gaps
- No independent evaluation was found of how Scan and Share affected help desk staffing or waiting times; the government reports volumes, not wait-time reductions.
- No published data was found on AI chatbot accuracy or uptake in Indian public hospitals.
- Use of AI chatbots in private chains' front offices (beyond a chat widget) is not documented from primary sources.
- The PGIMER AI kiosk's launch date and current status are unconfirmed.
