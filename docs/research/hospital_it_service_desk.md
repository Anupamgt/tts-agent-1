# Hospital IT Help Desk / IT Service Desk (Healthcare IT Support)

Scope note: These notes come from 24 search and fetch calls. The best primary or near-primary sources are the KLAS 2024 report page, an HCTec job posting, the Indian Lok Sabha statement reported by The Tribune, the HHS HC3 alert as reported by Security Affairs, and a UP/NITI best-practice document. Several "how it differs" claims come from vendor-written content (CereCore in Becker's, HCI Group). These vendors sell clinical service desk and go-live services, so they are labelled as such. Some items in the question list have no source in this pass and are listed under Gaps instead of being stated as fact: PACS, nurse call, pagers, medical-device and biomed support, specific SLA and priority matrices, and staffing ratios.

## 1. What tasks does a hospital IT service desk handle?

### Takeaway
A hospital service desk handles ordinary end-user IT (password resets, Citrix and remote-desktop issues, desktops, printers). On top of that it gives Tier 1 support for the clinical application stack: the EHR (Epic, in most US sources), plus lab, pharmacy, ordering, registration and billing modules. A failure in these systems hits patient flow directly, for example when discharge papers or lab barcodes cannot print.

### Cited Findings
- An HCTec "Clinical Service Desk Analyst" posting (managed services, Brentwood, TN) describes the role as "Tier 1 support to patients, physicians, and hospital staff for EHR-related applications, mainly clinical software." The named First Call Resolution tasks are **password resets, Citrix troubleshooting and common desktop troubleshooting**. Routine support is 90% of the role and continuing education on the client's software is 10% — [HCTec job posting](https://hctec.breezy.hr/p/1830769d67db01-clinical-service-desk)
- Printing shows how a "simple" IT fault becomes clinical: problems printing discharge instructions can delay discharges and reduce patient satisfaction and throughput (vendor-authored, CereCore) — [Becker's Hospital Review, May 28 2024](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/strategy/foundational-principles-for-clinical-service-desk-that-drive-clinician-satisfaction-and-operational-efficiency.html)
- The AIIMS Delhi outage (Nov 2022) lists the systems a hospital desk must keep running. The NIC-run eHospital server carried OPD and IPD digital services: smart lab, billing, report generation and appointments. The **barcode system for lab samples** stopped working and patients could not register for OPD — [MediaNama, Nov 2022](https://www.medianama.com/2022/11/223-aiims-ransomware-attack/)
- The Ascension ransomware attack (May 2024) took down the EHR, the patient portal and virtual visits, some phone systems, the systems for ordering tests, medications and procedures, and pharmacy systems, which were "hit really hard" — [NPR, May 22 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/05/22/1252853931/amid-cybersecurity-crisis-at-ascension-hospitals-workflows-are-disrupted)
- An Indian public-hospital HMIS rollout (Uttar Pradesh, 792 facilities, NIC eHospital NextGen and CDAC eSushrut) listed the infrastructure it had to provision and support: **Wi-Fi, tablets, PCs, scanners, printers and DG (diesel generator) backup**. Integrations covered LIS (lab information system), EMR, pharmacy and PHR systems — [NITI for States / UP best-practice document, Jun 2025](https://www.nitiforstates.gov.in/public-assets/images/20250625_121028_best-practices-draft-scan-%26-share-%2B-hmis-implementation.pdf)
- During EHR go-lives, super-users are asked to watch for **workstation shortages** and to encourage polite sharing of devices among clinicians (vendor content) — [HCI Group blog](https://blog.thehcigroup.com/go-live-at-the-elbow-support)

### Inferences
- The task mix is wider than in a corporate desk. Alongside the usual identity, endpoint and printing tickets, a large share of tickets involve clinical workflows (orders, results, lab labels, discharge documents). That is why posts ask for Epic end-user or clinical experience rather than only general IT experience.
- Printing (wristbands, specimen labels, discharge papers) appears in both a US vendor source and an Indian incident (lab barcodes). It is a recurring patient-flow-critical category.

### Gaps
- No source was found in this pass that lists or measures support for **PACS/radiology imaging, nurse call systems, pagers/Vocera/clinical smartphones, workstations-on-wheels (WOWs), label printer fleets, or networked medical devices** (infusion pumps, monitors). It is also unclear where the line between IT and biomedical/clinical engineering falls. These are commonly described in practice, but no citation was obtained.
- No ticket-category breakdown was found (e.g. % of tickets that are password resets or EHR access).

## 2. How does it support clinicians differently (24/7, patient-safety priority, tiers, go-live support, informatics)?

### Takeaway
Hospital desks run 24/7/365 because care never stops. They staff Tier 1 with clinically literate analysts, often Epic-certified or with clinical end-user experience, and they push resolution to Tier 1 ("shift left") so clinicians are not left waiting on Tier 2/3. During EHR go-lives the desk is reinforced with at-the-elbow floor support, super-users and a command center.

### Cited Findings
- Coverage: the HCTec clinical service desk role requires "24/7/365" Tier 1 clinical support following ITIL processes and HDI customer-service guidelines. The analyst triages, resolves at Tier 1 or escalates to Tier 2 — [HCTec job posting](https://hctec.breezy.hr/p/1830769d67db01-clinical-service-desk)
- Requirements: 1 year of **Epic clinical experience as an end user, credentialed trainer or other clinical role**, plus 2 years of customer service. A 2-year healthcare degree is preferred and a high school diploma is the minimum — [HCTec job posting](https://hctec.breezy.hr/p/1830769d67db01-clinical-service-desk)
- CereCore (an IT services firm owned by HCA Healthcare) presents the "clinical service desk" as a development of traditional IT support built for Epic. Its analysts gain Epic certifications in specific workflows, and Level 1 analysts shadow Level 2 analysts to learn end-user pain points. The strategy is to "shift left" from L2/L3 to L1 — [Becker's Hospital Review (CereCore authors), May 28 2024](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/strategy/foundational-principles-for-clinical-service-desk-that-drive-clinician-satisfaction-and-operational-efficiency.html)
- Pilot data (vendor-reported, HCA Healthcare Epic facilities): **35% fewer incidents** reached Epic Level 2 teams compared with traditional IT service desk support, and resolution was **30% faster**. Before the change, L2/L3 analysts spent **about 50% of their time** on issues that could have been handled earlier. First contact resolution is named as a KPI but no figure is given — [Becker's Hospital Review (CereCore), May 28 2024](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/strategy/foundational-principles-for-clinical-service-desk-that-drive-clinician-satisfaction-and-operational-efficiency.html)
- EHR go-live support (vendor content): set up a command center with knowledgeable analysts and keep them co-located. Put support staff and super-users throughout the facility. Agree support and escalation procedures before launch. Users report issues by calling the central help desk, filling a **fallback paper form**, or finding a nearby super-user. At-the-elbow staff watch the user's current workflow and walk them through it on the new system — [HCI Group blog](https://blog.thehcigroup.com/go-live-at-the-elbow-support)

### Inferences
- The main difference from a corporate desk is the cost of delay. An unresolved clinical-app ticket can hold up a discharge, a lab result or a medication order (see Section 1). Hospital desks therefore invest in clinical knowledge at Tier 1 instead of routing everything to application teams.
- "Clinical service desk" is a recognisable label for an Epic-literate Tier 1 desk, used by outsourcers such as HCTec and CereCore.

### Gaps
- No source here gives a **patient-safety priority matrix**, such as P1 when a clinical system is down in the ED or ICU, or specific response-time SLAs used by hospitals.
- No source found on **staff-to-user ratios** for at-the-elbow support or typical go-live support durations.
- **Clinical informatics roles** (CMIO, CNIO, nurse informaticists, Epic analysts) and how they link to the service desk were not covered by a fetched source.

## 3. What frameworks are used (ITIL, SLAs), and what downtime procedures exist?

### Takeaway
Hospital desks run on ITIL processes and SLAs, like corporate desks, and outsourcers are judged on meeting those SLAs. What hospitals add is a tested downtime mode: paper orders, couriers, manual registration and record-keeping. The desk has to support this mode when the EHR/HIS goes down, and recent ransomware events show it can last weeks.

### Cited Findings
- ITIL and HDI are named as the operating frameworks, and analysts must "meet and exceed established SLAs" — [HCTec job posting](https://hctec.breezy.hr/p/1830769d67db01-clinical-service-desk)
- KLAS rates outsourced healthcare help desks partly on SLA performance. CTG clients "generally say it meets SLAs, and a couple say it exceeds them." CereCore is praised for first-call resolution and for notifying clients of changes in IT performance metrics — [KLAS, Managed IT Services 2024 (Apr 3 2024)](https://klasresearch.com/report/managed-it-services-2024-which-firms-are-best-equipped-to-fill-help-desk-and-ams-needs/3247)
- Ascension downtime (from May 8 2024): staff hand-wrote medication, imaging and lab orders at the bedside, and **couriers** carried them to other departments. Each step took longer. Disruption was still ongoing about two weeks later with no restoration timeline. A nurse at Ascension Seton (Austin) said the hospital "just wasn't prepared" for the switch to paper: "It's like we went back 20 years, but not even with the tools we had then" — [NPR, May 22 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/05/22/1252853931/amid-cybersecurity-crisis-at-ascension-hospitals-workflows-are-disrupted)
- AIIMS Delhi downtime (Nov 2022): services moved to manual mode from 7:30 pm on 23 Nov 2022 — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2022/11/223-aiims-ransomware-attack/). Per the government, day-to-day operations, surgeries and record-keeping were done **manually**. Most e-Hospital functions (registration, appointments, admission, discharge) came back **about two weeks** after the attack, restored from an unaffected backup onto new servers — [The Tribune, Dec 16 2022](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/delhi/aiims-cyber-attack-no-ransom-was-demanded-most-data-restored-government-states-in-parliament-461633)
- A fallback paper form for reporting problems is recommended as part of go-live escalation planning — [HCI Group blog](https://blog.thehcigroup.com/go-live-at-the-elbow-support)

### Inferences
- In both the Ascension and AIIMS cases, downtime lasted about two weeks or more. Hospital desk planning therefore has to cover sustained manual operation, not just short outages. That includes back-entry of paper records once systems return, which is a likely surge in desk workload (inference, not sourced).

### Gaps
- No source fetched on formal **downtime toolkits**, such as read-only downtime PCs (e.g. Epic BCA), downtime report printing, or recovery/back-entry procedures, or on how ITIL change management is adapted for clinical systems (e.g. change freezes, scheduled downtime windows at low-census hours).
- No hospital-specific SLA numbers (speed-to-answer, abandonment rate, FCR %) were found.

## 4. What security and privacy responsibilities does it carry?

### Takeaway
The hospital help desk is a frontline security control. In 2024 the US HHS HC3 warned that attackers were calling healthcare help desks posing as staff to get MFA re-enrolled and then divert payments. The recommended response was callback-to-number-on-record and supervisor verification. Privacy law (HIPAA in the US; India's DPDP Act 2023 with the DPDP Rules 2025) and incidents like AIIMS Delhi 2022 set the stakes.

### Cited Findings
- HHS HC3 alert (reported April 2024): attackers called IT help desks from local area codes, posing as employees in financial or revenue-cycle roles. They supplied the last four SSN digits, corporate ID and demographic details (likely from professional networking sites and OSINT), and claimed a broken phone so the desk would **enroll a new device in MFA**. They then changed ACH details on payer websites and used compromised email to redirect payments to attacker-controlled US accounts, moving funds overseas. Some attempts used **AI voice cloning**. HHS compared the tactics to Scattered Spider (UNC3944), which hit a hospitality/entertainment firm in Sept 2023 with ALPHV ransomware, and noted UNC3944 had not claimed health-sector attacks — [Security Affairs, Apr 8 2024](https://securityaffairs.com/posts/healthcare-it-help-desks-attacks); see also [Data Privacy + Security Insider, Apr 2024](https://www.dataprivacyandsecurityinsider.com/2024/04/hc3-warns-health-sector-about-social-engineering-attacks-against-it-help-desks/)
- HC3 recommended mitigations: (1) **callbacks to the phone number on record** before password resets or new-device enrollment; (2) monitor for suspicious ACH changes and revalidate users with payer-website access; (3) **contact the employee's supervisor** to verify such requests; (4) train help desk staff to recognise social engineering and verify caller identity — [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/posts/healthcare-it-help-desks-attacks)
- HIPAA: the clinical service desk job requires a "basic understanding of healthcare regulations such as HIPAA" — [HCTec job posting](https://hctec.breezy.hr/p/1830769d67db01-clinical-service-desk)
- AIIMS Delhi (23 Nov 2022): the NIC identified ransomware. The main data server and a backup were breached, and a second backup with no infected files was disconnected as a precaution. CERT-In support was sought — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2022/11/223-aiims-ransomware-attack/). The government told Parliament that **five physical servers** hosting NIC's e-Hospital were affected and that "no specific amount of ransom was demanded." An FIR was filed with Delhi Police Special Cell. AIIMS then introduced **endpoint hardening, firewall policy changes and network segmentation** — [The Tribune, Dec 16 2022](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/delhi/aiims-cyber-attack-no-ransom-was-demanded-most-data-restored-government-states-in-parliament-461633). The government told the Rajya Sabha that about **1.3 TB of data was encrypted** — [The Wire, Dec 16 2022](https://thewire.in/government/aiims-servers-cyberattack-ransomware-rajya-sabha)
- Conflicting/unverified: media reports citing investigators said records of "nearly 3–4 crore patients" were potentially compromised — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/government/aiims-servers-cyberattack-ransomware-rajya-sabha). The government did not confirm this in the excerpts found. Early reports of a ₹200 crore ransom demand were denied, and the government said no specific ransom was demanded — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2022/11/223-aiims-ransomware-attack/), [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/delhi/aiims-cyber-attack-no-ransom-was-demanded-most-data-restored-government-states-in-parliament-461633)
- India DPDP Rules 2025 (notified by MeitY, reported Nov 14 2025): phased rollout, with Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 in force immediately, the Consent Manager rule at 12 months, and most obligations (Rules 3, 5–16, 22, 23) at 18 months. A breach must be reported to the Data Protection Board immediately, with a detailed report within **72 hours**, and affected individuals must be told promptly. Rule 6 safeguards cover encryption/masking/tokenisation, **access controls and activity logs**, **one-year log retention**, and backup/continuity. Some exemptions for healthcare providers apply to children's-data consent — [Storyboard18, Nov 14 2025](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/breaking-govt-notifies-final-dpdp-rules-sets-staggered-rollout-with-key-obligations-for-data-fiduciaries-84201.htm)

### Inferences
- The HC3 alert shows the help desk's identity-verification script is itself a security control. Knowledge-based checks (last-4 SSN, employee ID) were beaten with OSINT and breach data, which pushes hospitals toward callbacks, supervisor confirmation and stronger proofing for MFA resets.
- Under DPDP, Indian hospital desks will likely log access grants and resets more carefully and keep those logs (Rule 6's one-year retention and access-control requirements). They will also become part of the 72-hour breach-reporting chain. This is an inference: no source describes how hospitals are putting it into practice.

### Gaps
- No primary HHS HC3 document was fetched. Mitigations are as reported by Security Affairs. The exact alert date is unclear (reported around early April 2024).
- HIPAA Security Rule specifics (unique user IDs, emergency/"break-glass" access, audit controls) were not sourced in this pass.
- DPDP penalty amounts (commonly reported as up to ₹250 crore per breach of security safeguards) were **not verified** in the fetched sources.
- No source on how Indian hospitals verify identity for HIS password resets.

## 5. Is it in-house or outsourced? Typical staffing, skills and job titles

### Takeaway
Both models are common in the US. Outsourcing the help desk and application management services (AMS) to specialist firms is driven mainly by shortages of certified (e.g. Epic) staff and cost pressure. Mid-size systems (200–1,000 beds) are the most likely to increase outsourcing. Staff turnover is the recurring weak point in both models.

### Cited Findings
- KLAS "Managed IT Services 2024" (Apr 3 2024) evaluated outsourced help desk/AMS firms: Pivot Point Consulting, CereCore, GAVS Technologies, CTG, Nordic, Tegria, HCTec, HCLTech (limited data) and Oracle Health (limited data) — [KLAS](https://klasresearch.com/report/managed-it-services-2024-which-firms-are-best-equipped-to-fill-help-desk-and-ams-needs/3247)
- Why organisations outsource: **staffing shortages** are the main driver, especially finding certified resources. Other reasons are **cost pressure** and freeing internal IT for strategy and innovation rather than routine upkeep — [KLAS](https://klasresearch.com/report/managed-it-services-2024-which-firms-are-best-equipped-to-fill-help-desk-and-ams-needs/3247)
- Outlook (next 2–3 years): most will maintain current use. Increases come mostly from midsize organisations (200–1,000 beds); decreases from small (<200 beds) or very large (1,001+ beds) organisations, citing cost — [KLAS](https://klasresearch.com/report/managed-it-services-2024-which-firms-are-best-equipped-to-fill-help-desk-and-ams-needs/3247)
- Vendor quality notes: CTG had the highest end-user help desk satisfaction. HCTec had the lowest (limited data), with clients citing turnover and screening. Nordic's satisfaction fell 8 points (out of 100) after acquisition-related staff changes. Oracle Health was least satisfactory, attributed by clients to COVID-19 and the Cerner acquisition. GAVS clients cited offshoring as a challenge — [KLAS](https://klasresearch.com/report/managed-it-services-2024-which-firms-are-best-equipped-to-fill-help-desk-and-ams-needs/3247)
- Workforce problems in clinical service desks: high turnover, poor morale, inadequate training and limited career paths. Building in-house "requires significant, intentional investment" and a talent pipeline, while outsourcing needs a partnership approach and an ROI check (vendor view) — [Becker's (CereCore), May 28 2024](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/strategy/foundational-principles-for-clinical-service-desk-that-drive-clinician-satisfaction-and-operational-efficiency.html)
- Typical Tier 1 profile and title: "Clinical Service Desk Analyst". Needs Epic clinical end-user/trainer experience, 2 years of customer service, call-center experience preferred, HIPAA awareness, and a high school diploma minimum. The role is 24/7/365 — [HCTec](https://hctec.breezy.hr/p/1830769d67db01-clinical-service-desk)
- Indian public sector example (UP HMIS/ABDM): support is organised as a **State PMU technical team** of Project Managers (IT), Security Officers, HMIS Experts, Analysts, Registry Officers and Divisional Coordinators. New **ABDM Nodal Officers** were hired, and registration clerks, hospital admin staff and facility IT staff were reused. Data entry operators (DEOs) and registration-desk staff were trained — [NITI/UP best-practice doc](https://www.nitiforstates.gov.in/public-assets/images/20250625_121028_best-practices-draft-scan-%26-share-%2B-hmis-implementation.pdf)
- AIIMS Delhi's e-Hospital application was hosted and run by NIC, the central government's IT agency. NIC led restoration — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2022/11/223-aiims-ransomware-attack/); [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/delhi/aiims-cyber-attack-no-ransom-was-demanded-most-data-restored-government-states-in-parliament-461633)

### Inferences
- US outsourcing is a specialist healthcare market rated by KLAS, distinct from generic IT outsourcing. Some outsourcers are owned by health systems: CereCore is HCA's IT services company.
- In Indian government hospitals the "help desk" is often spread across the HMIS provider (NIC/CDAC), a state PMU, and facility-level IT staff, DEOs and clerks. This differs from the single tiered service desk model in US systems.

### Gaps
- No data on typical **staffing ratios** (analysts per bed or per user), salaries, or the in-house vs outsourced split across US or Indian hospitals.
- No Indian private-hospital examples (e.g. Apollo, Fortis, Manipal, Narayana) of help desk structure or outsourcing partners were found.
- No sourced list of standard job titles beyond "Clinical Service Desk Analyst", HMIS Expert and ABDM Nodal Officer (e.g. desktop support technician, Epic analyst, clinical informatics specialist).

## 6. Indian context: how do HIS and ABDM integration affect IT help desk work?

### Takeaway
ABDM is adding a layer of compliance- and integration-driven support work for Indian hospital IT. Medical colleges are now mandated (NMC, 2026) to run ABDM-compliant HMIS with ABHA linkage and HFR IDs, and states rolling out NIC eHospital or CDAC eSushrut rely on on-site handholding, daily training and WhatsApp groups for fast issue resolution. The hard problems are vendor integration, data quality (duplicate HFR records, weak record linkage), infrastructure gaps and staff resistance, more than tickets.

### Cited Findings
- NMC Office Memorandum (17 July 2026) directs medical colleges to implement an **ABDM-compliant HMIS** in attached hospitals "immediately". Earlier notices (28 Feb and 3 Mar 2026) required HFR ID submission and linking HMIS to the NHA's ABDM-HMIS portal. Requirements named: ABHA integration (M2), HFR/HPR mapping, advanced data sharing (M3), complete data in Health Record Linkage and/or Scan & Share. As of 10 July 2026, **247 colleges** used ABDM-enabled software but showed no data on the dashboard (one of three non-compliance categories). MARB "may initiate further action." Colleges must also integrate AEBAS biometric attendance and CCTV with NMC — [Medical Dialogues, Jul 18 2026](https://medicaldialogues.in/health-news/nmc/nmc-flags-non-compliant-medical-colleges-over-mandatory-abdm-hmis-integration-warns-of-action-175264)
- UP rollout: started at KGMU and grew to **792 public facilities** using NIC eHospital NextGen and CDAC eSushrut. Support included on-site handholding and activation in all districts, district and onsite training, **daily virtual training sessions and YouTube Live**, a **real-time dashboard** tracking onboarding issues, and **WhatsApp groups for peer learning and fast issue resolution**. No dedicated help desk is mentioned. Reported outputs: 2.27+ crore Scan & Share OPD tokens, 6.21+ crore health records linked with ABHA, 13.59+ crore ABHA accounts — [NITI/UP best-practice doc, Jun 2025](https://www.nitiforstates.gov.in/public-assets/images/20250625_121028_best-practices-draft-scan-%26-share-%2B-hmis-implementation.pdf)
- UP challenges and fixes: staff resistance (sensitisation workshops, demos); infrastructure gaps in Wi-Fi, tablets, PCs, scanners, printers and DG backup (phased assessment and provisioning); **duplicate HFR records** (deduplication with the NHA HFR team); **inconsistent record linkage** (stronger validation in HMIS-ABDM APIs, audit-trail reviews) — [NITI/UP best-practice doc](https://www.nitiforstates.gov.in/public-assets/images/20250625_121028_best-practices-draft-scan-%26-share-%2B-hmis-implementation.pdf)
- National picture (HMPI, Jul 2026): **880+ million ABHA accounts** as of May 2026, inflated partly by about 130 million CoWIN-seeded accounts. 500,000+ HFR facilities and 860,000+ HPR professionals. M3 (cross-facility record retrieval) use is "extremely low", and Rajasthan's ABHA-to-EHR linkage is below 20%. Many facilities run proprietary software with no ABDM-compliant API layer, and integration costs for mid-sized providers are estimated at **INR 30–50 lakh**. Workflow steps can add time in 2–5 minute consultations. The paper calls the gap "a training problem as much as an incentive problem" — [Health Management, Policy & Innovation (HMPI), Jul 9 2026](https://hmpi.org/2026/07/09/from-infrastructure-to-impact-operationalizing-indias-ayushman-bharat-digital-mission/)
- Scan & Share redistributes congestion to specialist referral desks rather than removing it — [HMPI](https://hmpi.org/2026/07/09/from-infrastructure-to-impact-operationalizing-indias-ayushman-bharat-digital-mission/)
- Discrepancy: the UP document reports "81+ crore ABHA addresses" alongside 13.59 crore accounts. This looks inconsistent and should be checked against the NHA dashboard — [NITI/UP best-practice doc](https://www.nitiforstates.gov.in/public-assets/images/20250625_121028_best-practices-draft-scan-%26-share-%2B-hmis-implementation.pdf)

### Inferences
- In India, desk work tied to ABDM is less about classic incident tickets and more about onboarding, data-quality fixes (HFR/HPR mapping, ABHA linkage errors), consent-flow questions from front desks, and keeping dashboards populated for regulator compliance (NMC/MARB).
- The informal support channels used in UP (WhatsApp groups, YouTube Live training) point to a less formalised, ITIL-light support model in Indian public hospitals than in US health systems. No source gives a counter-example.
- The AIIMS 2022 attack showed that centralised NIC-hosted HIS creates single points of failure. Afterwards AIIMS moved to network segmentation and endpoint hardening, which are measures that change how local IT teams manage endpoints and access.

### Gaps
- No source on **private Indian hospital** HIS help desks (e.g. vendors such as KareXpert, Insta by Practo, or in-house systems at Apollo, Fortis or Max) or their support SLAs.
- No source on an NHA/ABDM central helpdesk or how facility IT escalates ABDM sandbox/production integration issues. A Scan & Share onboarding manual exists on abdm.gov.in but was not fetched.
- No Indian job postings for hospital IT help desk/HIS support roles were reviewed (titles, pay, shifts).
